**Primary audience:** users evaluating AI-assisted market-analysis/scoring tools who want to understand data sources, score logic and limitations

## 1. User problem

A user sees AI scores, confidence values and market-analysis claims across finance products but cannot easily tell:

- what the score actually measures;
- which data and evidence feed it;
- whether one formula is reused across incompatible asset classes;
- what happens when source data is missing or stale;
- which AI/research signals alter the canonical score and which remain separate context;
- where analysis ends and personalized investment advice would begin.

The content must answer those questions without turning CAPITAL-AI into a black-box prediction product.

## 2. Search / intent evidence

Current public SERP/product evidence shows recurring demand around:

- `KI Aktienanalyse`;
- `Aktienscore` / `Scoring-Modell`;
- scoring factors and methodology;
- AI/data-source transparency;
- multi-asset AI analysis;
- model limits, confidence and non-advisory boundaries.

This brief uses those themes qualitatively. **No search volume, ranking position, keyword difficulty, traffic estimate or conversion forecast is asserted.**

## 3. Core proposition

CAPITAL-AI should explain its analysis as a governed evidence chain rather than as one “magic AI score”:

`provider observation → provenance/data-quality evidence → model-specific features → governed scoring contract → explainable result`

Asset classes and model families may use different versioned contracts. Public content must never collapse them into a fabricated universal formula.

## 4. FINTECH truth gates before publication

`CAPITAL-AI-FINTECH` must approve the exact public-safe statements for every model/version referenced.

Required review evidence:

1. exact productive scoring/model version(s);
2. factor/feature set for each asset/model actually discussed;
3. provider/evidence lineage that may be exposed publicly;
4. data-quality/freshness semantics;
5. exact missing-factor behavior for the referenced contract;
6. whether sentiment, momentum, pattern or AI summaries are canonical score inputs or separate research lenses;
7. backtest/correlation/model-promotion state where any performance interpretation is discussed.

Do **not** publish historical/superseded research weights as current scoring truth.

## 5. Source-backed CAPITAL-AI boundaries

Repository evidence supports these high-level content directions, subject to FINTECH review:

- CAPITAL-AI spans multiple asset classes rather than one stock-only surface.
- Explainability/evidence includes provenance, Evidence IDs and feature/scoring lineage.
- Versioned crypto scoring/model contracts exist.
- Traditional technical scoring can exclude a missing factor and renormalize remaining weights where the exact applicable contract specifies this behavior.
- Some research lenses can remain separate from the canonical score instead of silently changing it.
- New subclass/research models require versioned features, verified provider coverage and governed promotion before they become productive scoring truth.

## 6. Recommended content structure

### H1 candidate

**Wie funktioniert KI-Scoring bei Marktanalysen? Daten, Faktoren und Grenzen verständlich erklärt**

### Sections

1. **Was ein Markt-Score ist — und was nicht**
   - decision-support / research classification;
   - not a guaranteed forecast;
   - not personalized investment advice.

2. **Warum es nicht den einen Score für jede Anlageklasse gibt**
   - equities, crypto, forex, commodities and other domains have different evidence/model needs;
   - shared interface != universal formula.

3. **Vom Datenpunkt zur Bewertung**
   - provider/source observation;
   - provenance and timestamps;
   - data-quality/freshness checks;
   - feature extraction;
   - versioned scoring contract.

4. **Was bei fehlenden Daten passiert**
   - explain exact contract behavior only;
   - missing/unverified evidence must not be silently invented.

5. **Wie Explainability sichtbar wird**
   - evidence/source lineage;
   - factor/feature context;
   - model/version identity where public-safe.

6. **Score vs. zusätzliche Research-Linsen**
   - distinguish canonical score from separate sentiment/momentum/pattern/AI context where applicable.

7. **Sieben Fragen, die Nutzer jedem AI-Scoring-Tool stellen sollten**
   - Welche Datenquelle?
   - Wie aktuell?
   - Welche Faktoren?
   - Welche Modellversion?
   - Was passiert bei fehlenden Daten?
   - Was bedeutet der Score ausdrücklich nicht?
   - Kann ich die Herleitung nachvollziehen?

8. **Grenzen und menschliche Entscheidung**
   - model/data errors remain possible;
   - no performance guarantee;
   - no personalized investment advice.
